Scar Threshold

How hard it has to rain before a burned canyon lets go

Point at a basin to see its numbers.

How this was made

Sentinel-2 imagery gives burn severity, a 10 m USGS elevation model gives slope and drainage, and the USDA soil survey gives erodibility. Those three feed the USGS M1 model, which returns the rainfall each basin needs for a 50% chance of producing a debris flow.

The model implementation matches the official USGS package exactly, and reproduces the published USGS assessment for the Bridge Fire to floating point precision when given their inputs. Three of the fires here have been compared against field-mapped soil burn severity, and on all three this pipeline runs conservative: it over-warns rather than under-warns.

A fire that BAER has not yet assessed still gets a map, because the rainfall that matters arrives before the field survey does. Those fires are labelled, and their numbers carry an uncertainty range but no accuracy check.

"High" means at least a 60% chance, which is where the top two of the five likelihood classes USGS publishes begin.

Code and full method

Not an operational warning product. This is a portfolio project. It reproduces a published USGS model from public data and has not been reviewed or endorsed by USGS, CAL FIRE, the US Forest Service or any other agency. Nothing here predicts whether a particular canyon will produce a debris flow, and it must not be used for evacuation, access or any other safety decision. For official post-fire hazard information see the USGS Landslide Hazards Program and your county emergency management agency.
